Koko is a chatbot that fits on a Game Boy Advance cartridge. Type a message and Koko, a friendly husky, writes back at about three words a second. No internet, no helper chip: the language model itself is the game.

Play it above in your browser, or download the ROM for an emulator or flash cartridge. The browser version runs the same code and the same model on the same 240 x 160 screen. Only the keyboard is real.

What Koko does

  • Chats, tells short stories and jokes, and answers simple questions
  • Suggests your next word while you type
  • Answers "who was...?" and "what is...?" from a built-in table of 34,000 Wikipedia opening sentences
  • Does sums exactly

In blind comparisons, AI judges preferred Koko's chat replies to SmolLM2-135M-Instruct's on 18 of 20 messages, and its stories to GPT-2's on 19 of 20. On general knowledge Koko loses clearly: 22 of 60 questions right against SmolLM2's 43.

What it took to make

  • Training the weights on the cartridge: about 13 GPU-hours on RTX 5090s. This is the figure in the table above.
  • All training experiments: 19 runs, about 35 GPU-hours, plus GPU time for writing the training conversations.
  • AI coding assistant: the code, training setup and tests were written with Claude over three days.

Honest notes

  • Koko is tiny and often wrong.
  • Replies marked with a small book come from the fact table, and those with an equals box are calculated. Everything else is the model. Press the book key to turn the helpers off.
  • It understands plain English letters, digits and basic punctuation, and remembers about the last 190 words.
  • The judges were AI, not people, and the test messages were the kind Koko was trained for. Sizes for the other models are their full 32-bit files.
  • It has run on a real Game Boy Advance from a flash cartridge, and is tested in the mGBA emulator.
