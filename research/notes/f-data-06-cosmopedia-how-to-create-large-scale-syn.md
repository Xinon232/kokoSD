# Cosmopedia: how to create large-scale synthetic data for pre-training

URL: https://huggingface.co/blog/cosmopedia (published 20 March 2024). Authors: Loubna Ben Allal, Anton Lozhkov, Daniel van Strien (Hugging Face). Dataset citation also lists Guilherme Penedo, Thomas Wolf, Leandro von Werra.
Read in primary form: the blog post [B], the dataset card [C], and source files in github.com/huggingface/cosmopedia [R]. Lines marked [mine] are my own arithmetic.

## What it is
An open reproduction attempt of the Phi-1.5 synthetic pre-training set: 31.06M documents / about 25B tokens of synthetic textbooks, blog posts, stories, forum posts and WikiHow articles, all generated by Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-v0.1. It is a data-engineering write-up, not a paper: prompts, generation script, dedup and decontamination code are released (Apache-2.0). The main lesson is that diversity is bought by conditioning every prompt on a distinct "seed sample" plus an audience and style, and that most of the effort went into prompt iteration, not compute. A 1B Llama-2-architecture model (cosmo-1b) was trained on it as a quality check.

## Mechanism
Prompt = template(style, audience) + seed extract (+ topic).
- Seed extract: first 1000 characters of the seed document (`EXTRACT_SIZE = 1000`) [R].
- Topic: for web seeds, appended as `, within the context of "<topic>"` with probability 0.5 (`random.random() < 0.5`) in web_samples_v2; 100% in v1 [R, C].
- Topics: cluster millions of web documents (text-clustering repo) into 145 clusters; label each by showing Mixtral extracts of 10 random members and asking for the common topic plus an educational score out of 10; the score only separates the extremes, so clusters were inspected by hand and 35 dropped (card: 145 - 35; blog says 112 kept; the two do not add up exactly) [B, C].
- Audiences: young children, high-school students, college students, researchers. Styles: textbook (academic and narrative), blog post, WikiHow. Up to 12x prompts per seed [B].
- Story styles (seeded from UltraChat "questions about the world" and filtered OpenHermes-2.5 first turns): young_children_story, morality_story, problem_solving_story, forums_story, reddit_post [R].
- Young-children story template, verbatim essentials [R]: "Write an educational story (3-5 paragraphs) targeted at young children using simple words. The story should be inspired from this text snippet: <EXTRACT> ... - Dialogue: include at least one dialogue and insightful conversation. - Unexpected twist ... Do not start with classic sentences like "Once upon a time", be creative."
- Sampling [R, generation script defaults]: max_new_tokens 2500, temperature 0.6, top_p 0.95, top_k 50, repetition_penalty 1.2; llm-swarm launching TGI instances on Slurm, async requests bounded by a semaphore, checkpoint every 500-1000 samples.
- Dedup [R]: datatrove MinHash with default `MinhashConfig()` (signature -> buckets -> cluster -> filter).
- Decontamination [R]: lowercase, tokenize with `\w+`, index all benchmark 10-grams in a dict; any document sharing a 10-gram is a candidate; then `difflib.SequenceMatcher(None, a.lower(), b.lower(), autojunk=False)`; drop if len(matched substrings)/len(benchmark sample) > 0.5.

## Numbers
- 31,064,744 rows; about 25B tokens (Mistral-7B tokenizer) [C]. About 805 tokens/doc [mine].
- Splits [C metadata]: web_samples_v1 12,426,348; web_samples_v2 10,345,867; stories 4,992,964; auto_math_text 1,949,895; stanford 1,020,024; wikihow 179,191; openstax 126,332; khanacademy 24,123. Web seeds are over 80% of prompts, 23M [B].
- Seed scarcity: only 16,000 OpenStax units and 250,000 Stanford units [B].
- Compute: "over 10k" H100 GPU-hours [B, R]. Implies at most about 2.5M tokens per H100-hour, roughly 700 tokens/s per GPU [mine].
- Duplicates: under 1% of files flagged by MinHash [C, R].
- Contamination removed (samples, unique benchmark items) [C]: web+stanford+openstax: ARC-Easy 30 (13), ARC-Challenge 19 (3), BoolQ 386 (41), HellaSwag 6 (5), PIQA 5 (3); stories: ARC-Easy 33 (20), ARC-Challenge 20 (12), BoolQ 27 (21). So contamination is tiny but nonzero even in synthetic text.
- cosmo-1b: better than TinyLlama-1.1B on ARC-easy, ARC-challenge, OpenBookQA, MMLU; comparable to Qwen-1.5-1B on two; worse than Phi-1.5 [B, Figure 10; the bar values are in an image I could not read].

## Smallest scale tested, and whether the result held there
Only one student size, about 1B parameters (the card says 1.8B in one place). Nothing at 1-100M parameters. The diversity findings were observed on "a few hundred samples per prompt" and then held at 30M documents (<1% MinHash duplicates). Whether textbook-style data helps a 5-30M-parameter model is not tested here.

## Training recipe and gotchas
- Naive audience/style swaps ("...for young children" vs "...blog post") still produced a high duplicate rate; the template must spell out how content and format differ per audience [B].
- Stock openings: stories always began "Once upon a time" / "The sun hung low in the sky", forum posts "A few years back". Naming the banned openings reduced but did not remove them [B, C].
- v2 prompts ask for no title and no introduction, and depth over breadth, because titles and intros were redundant across samples [C].
- Textbook-only models lacked grade-school common sense; the stories split (16% of rows) was added to fix this [B].
- Mixtral hallucinates history and math; authors suggest RAG from Wikipedia [B].
- MinHash only catches near-verbatim duplicates; it says nothing about template-level sameness (shared plot arcs, shared phrases).

## Mapping onto the GBA design
Zero run-time cost: this is a data recipe. No lookups, adds, multiplies, RAM or ROM are involved. Indirect effects:
- Budget [mine]: at about 700 tokens/s on an H100 for a 47B MoE, a 5090 running a 7-14B open model under vLLM should be several times faster per GPU; 300M-1B tokens of generated simple English is plausible in tens of GPU-hours. We need about 1/50 of Cosmopedia at most.
- What to change: replace the 4 audiences with a single simple-English register plus a vocabulary constraint tied to our 16K tokenizer; replace textbook styles with dialogue styles (question/answer, small talk, explanation for a child, name recall, short stories with dialogue). Keep seed conditioning: seeds = Simple Wikipedia paragraphs, UltraChat/OpenHermes first turns, TinyStories prompts.
- Stock openings matter more for us: hashed n-gram tables in ROM will memorise any over-represented 3-5-gram opener and predictive typing will suggest it. Add a post-hoc check: count the first 5 tokens of every sample and cap any opener above a set share.
- Decontamination recipe applies directly to our GPT-2 comparison sets (BLiMP, EWoK, any held-out chat eval): 10-gram index + SequenceMatcher > 0.5.

## Verdict
Adopt as the generation-pipeline template (seed x style x audience grid, 1000-char seed extract, topic 50%, banned-opener clause, few-hundred-sample pilot per template, MinHash dedup, 10-gram decontamination). Do not adopt the content mix: textbooks for researchers are useless for a tiny simple-English chatbot. Settling experiment: generate 3 pilots of 2,000 samples each (no seed, seed only, seed + style/audience instructions), measure MinHash duplicate rate, distinct-4-gram ratio and top-20 opener share; then train the same small model on 50M tokens of each and compare held-out loss on real simple-English text.
